Why a BJT amplifies

Think of the emitter as a carrier launcher, the base as a thin gate, and the collector as a strong vacuum cleaner. The base-emitter junction is forward biased, so carriers are injected. The base is thin, so most carriers do not recombine there. The collector-base junction is reverse biased, so its electric field sweeps carriers into the collector.

Not two separate diodes: transistor action needs the two junctions to be physically coupled through a thin base. If you separate the two PN junctions, the collector cannot “catch” carriers injected by the emitter.

Why biasing matters

The BJT exponential equation is powerful, but dangerous: small changes in VBE can create big current changes. Also, β, VBE, and VCE,sat change with process, supply, and temperature.

PVT: Process, Voltage, Temperature. At constant IC, VBE typically changes by about −2 mV/°C. β also varies with temperature and collector current.

Good bias design uses a voltage divider for base voltage and an emitter resistor for negative feedback:

VE ≈ VB − 0.7,   IE ≈ VE/RE
Emitter resistor intuition: if current rises, VE rises, so VBE falls. That pushes current back down. It self-corrects.

Exam-solving checklist

1. Identify analysis typeFor this lecture: DC / large-signal Q-point analysis.
2. Assume active firstUse VBE ≈ 0.7 V and IC = βIB.
3. Solve currents and node voltagesFind IE, IB, IC, VE, VC.
4. Check VCEVCE = VC − VE. If VCE > VCE,sat, active is valid.
5. If active fails, redo as saturationUse VBE ≈ 0.7 V and VCE ≈ 0.2 V. Do not use IC=βIB.

Memory cards

Active mode: BEJ forward, BCJ reverse. Amplifier region. IC=βIB.
Saturation: both junctions forward. Switch-on region. IC is limited by the external circuit.
Cutoff: both junctions reverse. Switch-off region. IC≈0.
VCE check: if VCE becomes too small, the transistor cannot keep collector current controlled by β anymore.
Biasing: emitter resistor makes current stable; voltage divider makes base voltage less dependent on β.
Most dangerous mistake: calculating IC = βIB, getting impossible VCE, and still keeping the active-mode answer.
